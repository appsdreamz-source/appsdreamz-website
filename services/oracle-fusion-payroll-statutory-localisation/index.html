The problem

Localisation goes wrong without raising a flag

A gratuity base that excludes contract-eligible allowances does not throw an error. Payroll runs, the payslip looks right, and the end-of-service provision is understated every month until someone leaves and the accrual does not cover the settlement.

The same pattern sits in statutory files that pass validation but misclassify a pay component, in contribution bases that drift after a salary restructure, and in leave accrual that follows the calendar rather than the contract. None of it appears as a defect. All of it eventually appears as a number that has to be explained.

Who it is for

Payroll directors, finance controllers and HRIS leads in the GCC and India, and multinationals adding those countries.

Questions

Frequently asked

Which countries have you delivered?

The UAE, Saudi Arabia and India. The mechanism is the same in any jurisdiction, and we tell you on the first call what we have and have not done for yours.

Do you work with Oracle’s delivered localisations?

Yes. We start from them and design the element set from your employment contracts, so the configuration reflects your terms rather than a template.

Can you validate an existing WPS file?

Yes. We check the classification of each pay component in the file, not only that it passes validation, and reconcile it back to the payroll register.

How do you test a gratuity base?

We list which allowances each contract makes eligible, compare them with the elements feeding the base, and reconcile the end-of-service provision to the balance sheet.
